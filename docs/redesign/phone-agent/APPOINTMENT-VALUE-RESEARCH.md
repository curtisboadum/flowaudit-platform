# US dental appointment value: research for the 90-second overview

Researched 9 October 2026. Audience: US-first dental practice owners, with group relevance. This is contextual market research, not a survey of FlowAudit prospects, a fee recommendation or an ROI estimate.

## Selected benchmark

[CareCredit procedural-cost tables](https://www.carecredit.com/dentistry/costs/) publish a $50–$350 range and $203 national average for a routine exam including cleaning and X-rays. The footnote for this item identifies the 2023 Synchrony Average Procedural Cost Study conducted by ASQ360, October 2023 cost tables. CareCredit's separate dentistry overview combines 2023–2024 sourcing; the specific table's item footnote is the more precise attribution used here. Access date is 2026; the underlying study is not a 2026 price survey. The film uses the range with the source year, not a claim of measured current practice income.

## Cross-checks and variation

- [Delta Dental cleaning costs](https://www.deltadental.com/protect-my-smile/procedures/dental-cleanings/cost-and-insurance-coverage/): $85–$160 for a standard cleaning without benefits; $140–$220 for periodontal maintenance, $180–$295 per quadrant for scaling/root planing. These are different services, not interchangeable “appointment values.”
- [Delta Dental checkups](https://www.deltadental.com/protect-my-smile/visiting-the-dentist/what-happens-during-a-dental-checkup/): typical exam/cleaning appointments are 45–60 minutes; prices vary by coverage and network status. A practice also needs capacity to deliver any additional appointments.
- [Aspen new-patient offers](https://www.aspendental.com/pricing-offers/dental-discounts/): selected offices advertise a $29 exam/X-ray offer for eligible uninsured new adult patients, expiring 31 December 2026. This is a qualified promotion, not a national normal fee. [Aspen first-visit FAQ](https://www.aspendental.com/about/faq/) explicitly excludes cleaning from the first exam. This prevents conflating the demo's new-patient booking with a guaranteed bundled exam/cleaning/X-ray purchase.
- [ADA Medicaid schedules](https://www.ada.org/advocacy/advocacy-issues/medicaid/medicaid-fee-schedules): state-specific reimbursement is a separate category from self-pay fees. The archived 2025 Connecticut adult schedule lists $42.25 for comprehensive evaluation and $41.60 for adult prophylaxis; this historical payer example is not used as a current national benchmark or added into a visit bundle.

## Practice economics and buyer concerns

[ADA Q2 2026 dental economy report](https://www.ada.org/-/media/project/ada-organization/ada/ada-org/files/resources/research/hpi/state_us_dental_economy_q22026.pdf) identifies reimbursement pressures and continued recruitment difficulty, particularly for hygienists. It also shows mixed dentist attitudes toward AI and interest in front-desk functions. These findings support scoped team assistance and practice approval, without suggesting universal adoption, spare capacity or FlowAudit performance. The report uses distinct samples for different measures; no headline statistic is imported into the film.

[ADA practice collections guidance](https://pages.ada.org/am-i-ready-to-open-a-second-dental-practice-location) distinguishes adjusted production from collections and emphasizes overhead. A posted fee, adjusted production, cash collected and profit are different amounts. Patient copay is not total practice income. A higher-value treatment course can require several appointments, clinical indication, acceptance and payment; it cannot be attributed to every initial enquiry.

[ADA dental-care-market information](https://www.ada.org/resources/research/health-policy-institute/dental-care-market) reports that its Dental Fees Survey was discontinued in 2023. An alleged “2024/2025 ADA fee survey” is therefore not used. Older survey references are not silently treated as current research.

## Application and exclusions

The film identifies published patient costs and says this is a fee benchmark, not a revenue forecast. Owners are directed to their own attendance, collections and costs. The $50–$350 range does not describe exam-only visits, every treatment, PPO or Medicaid collections, profit, lifetime patient value or guaranteed additional revenue. Do not multiply missed calls by this range. Do not use crown/implant course prices as value per booked routine enquiry. No guaranteed recovered appointments, universal availability, insurance verification, treatment recommendation or collection capability is claimed.

UK and Canadian enquiries remain welcome on the existing page, with regional assessment; US dollar context is labelled US. No conversion to local tariffs or NHS revenue is inferred. Public FlowAudit pricing remains private.
